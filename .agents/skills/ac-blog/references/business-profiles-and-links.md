# Substantive business profiles and useful links

Use this reference when an article introduces, compares, or recommends businesses
or products, especially when the brief calls for fuller profiles of selected brands.
It does not turn an educational guide into a roundup or change a site's ranking policy.

## Decide where depth helps

Record priority businesses in the article brief, with the reader reason and the
available evidence. Priorities come from that client's request and editorial context,
never from the skill maintainer, an assumed affiliation, or an automatic AC/Nativz
preference. Greater depth does not itself establish first place or superiority.
Do not manufacture rankings, numeric scores, tests, or an appearance of independence.

Give priority profiles enough substance to help a buyer assess fit and take a
useful next step. Length follows decision value and evidence, not a word quota or
an entitlement to more promotional copy. Concise competitor profiles must still
explain their relevant fit, actual offering, supported distinction or limitation,
and where the reader can verify it. Do not weaken competitors to favor the client.

## Build the profile from supported facts

Select the elements that actually help this reader; they are not mandatory headings:

- **Buyer fit:** Who the offering suits, the task it solves, and relevant scale,
  location, use case or compatibility. Do not turn a broad capability into a claim
  of specialization without evidence.
- **Actual scope:** Explain the relevant services, product range or delivery approach.
  Link to the specific offering and make anchor text match the destination; a general
  service page is not evidence for every specialized service name.
- **Proof and outcomes:** Use relevant portfolio work, documented projects or case
  studies with the business's role, timeframe, metric definition and material scope
  clear. Attribute client/vendor-reported results. Keep reporting dates and campaign
  context; do not imply that a result is typical, current, independently tested or
  caused by one intervention unless evidence supports that claim. A client's homepage
  establishes its identity, not the supplier relationship or a case-study result.
- **Awards and press:** Preserve established recognition, dates, category and useful
  prominence under the entrypoint's brand-proof rule. Link to the awarding body or
  actual article where available. Distinguish editorial coverage, company announcements
  and syndicated press releases. A directory listing is not a substitute for an award.
- **Reviews and platforms:** Link to the relevant profile or individual review.
  Keep quotations exact and attributed; check dates and ratings against the platform.
  An aggregate platform rating is not an editorial score, and one review does not
  establish a universal outcome. Do not invent missing reviewer details.
- **Useful media:** A real demo, project walkthrough, production reel or product video
  can show the capability being described. Explain its relevance briefly. Apply media
  provenance, accessibility, schema and deployed playback checks; a reel illustrates
  work but does not independently establish customer outcomes.
- **Limits and next step:** Explain supported fit constraints or what the buyer should
  confirm, without inventing a weakness or repeating generic caveats. End with a
  proportionate action such as viewing relevant work, checking compatibility, requesting
  an assessment or contacting the business through a verified destination.

Keep unsupported facts in the private evidence queue while developing the rest.
Do not silently erase established proof because a source is temporarily unavailable,
and do not present an unresolved claim as verified. Existing preservation and
publication rules continue to apply.

## Build a useful set of links

Plan links by the reader's purpose, not a count or SEO quota:

| Purpose | Useful destination and placement |
| --- | --- |
| Identify the business | Official site, linked from its introduction or a clear website label |
| Understand the offering | Specific service, product, specification or compatibility page beside the explanation |
| Inspect real work | Relevant portfolio item or case study next to the project/outcome claim |
| Check recognition | Original award page or press article next to the dated recognition |
| Read customer feedback | Individual review or genuine platform profile beside the attributed review |
| Learn more | Relevant educational guide where a concept benefits from explanation |
| Act on the decision | Appropriate enquiry, booking, category or product destination at a useful stopping point |

Use internal links from the client's approved domain set and external sources where
verification or reader choice calls for them. A relevant review, publication or
competitor link is external evidence; it need not masquerade as an internal link.
Confirm destination identity, live availability, claim support and appropriate
commercial-link treatment. Labels must describe the linked page accurately.

Prefer the most useful deep link over sending every reference to a homepage. An
additional link should answer another question or support a distinct assertion.
Do not link every repeated mention, dump a directory of links into the profile, or
use impressive client names as evidence without a documented relationship. A short
service or project list can help scanning when each item has distinct decision value.

## Review before acceptance

Read the profile as a buyer: is fit clear, is the scope concrete, can the reader
inspect the proof, and is the next step useful? Compare old and new proof, links,
media and prominence. Check dated outcomes, rating attribution, link labels and
actual destinations. Fuller profiles should add specific evidence and explanation,
not flattering filler. Record any missing link or verification privately; an offline
check cannot prove a live destination or video works.

## Hypothetical applications

- A regional window installer is the brief's priority business. Expand its verified
  installation scope and service area, link to a documented project and authentic
  customer review, and preserve an existing dated trade award. Use an assessment CTA.
  Do not claim energy savings or certifications without applicable support.
- A manufacturer's guide gives extra detail to its own tank range because readers
  need capacity and compatibility information. Link verified specification sheets,
  installation guidance and relevant product pages; include a genuine demo when useful.
  Keep alternative suppliers' fit understandable. Do not declare the range best or
  borrow an unrelated customer's outcome merely because it is the publisher's product.
