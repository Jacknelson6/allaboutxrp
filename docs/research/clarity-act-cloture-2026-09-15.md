## What cloture means and what happens next

- Cloture limits further consideration and is the Senate mechanism for overcoming a filibuster. For legislation, it requires three-fifths of senators duly chosen and sworn, normally 60 in a fully seated 100-member Senate. [U.S. Senate explanation](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/filibusters-cloture.htm)
- Majority Leader John Thune filed cloture on August 8, 2026, specifically on the motion to proceed to H.R. 3633. [Senate floor activity record](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/LIS/floor_activity/08_07_2026_Senate_Floor.htm), [Senate cloture log](https://www.senate.gov/legislative/cloture/119.htm)
- If cloture succeeds, the Senate can spend up to 30 post-cloture hours on the motion to proceed and then dispose of that motion. Adoption of the motion to proceed puts the bill before the Senate. Cloture does not itself adopt the motion or pass the bill. [Rule XXII](https://www.rules.senate.gov/download/the-rules-of-the-senate), [CRS guide](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/PDF/RL30360/RL30360.22.pdf)
- Once the bill is before the Senate, senators may consider a substitute and other amendments. Additional cloture motions can be needed on amendments or the bill. Final passage generally requires a simple majority of senators present and voting, not invariably 51 votes. [Senate voting guide](https://www.senate.gov/about/powers-procedures/voting.htm), [CRS guide](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/PDF/RL30360/RL30360.22.pdf)
- A failed cloture vote would stall the current floor path, but it would not automatically erase the bill. Leadership could negotiate, seek reconsideration, or schedule another attempt. Do not call it legally “dead” solely because one cloture attempt fails. [CRS guide](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/RL/PDF/RL30360/RL30360.22.pdf)
- The Senate is considering a substantially revised substitute. If it passes a different version from the House, the House must accept the Senate language or the chambers must reconcile the texts before a bill can go to the president. [House legislative process](https://www.house.gov/the-house-explained/the-legislative-process/to-the-senate), [Senate glossary](https://www.senate.gov/about/research-tools/glossary.htm)

## What the CLARITY Act is

The CLARITY Act is a broad federal digital-asset market-structure proposal. It would define categories of crypto assets and transactions, divide oversight between the Securities and Exchange Commission and Commodity Futures Trading Commission, create a CFTC registration regime for spot digital-commodity intermediaries, establish issuer disclosure and fundraising pathways, add customer-property and anti-fraud rules, address illicit finance and DeFi, preserve self-custody and some software-developer protections, regulate crypto kiosks, address stablecoin rewards, and impose digital-asset ethics rules on covered federal officials. [September 10 substitute, table of contents and Divisions A to C](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)

### Core token-classification model

- A **network token** is a digital commodity linked to a distributed ledger system that derives, or is expected to derive, value from use of that system. Network tokens exclude traditional financial rights such as debt, equity, liquidation rights, and entitlements to payments from a person. The draft treats a qualifying network token as a non-security for federal securities-law purposes. [September 10 substitute, section 4B(a)(7) and 4B(b), PDF pages 22 to 28](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)
- An **ancillary asset** is a network token whose value depends on the entrepreneurial or managerial efforts of an originator. The draft can treat an originator-led offer as an investment-contract transaction while treating the token itself and compliant secondary-market transactions as non-securities. This maintains a distinction between an asset and the transaction or scheme through which it is sold. [September 10 substitute, sections 4B(a)(1) and 4B(b), PDF pages 13 to 30](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)
- Originators would face initial and periodic disclosures and insider disposition restrictions. The draft directs the SEC to create a tailored **Regulation Crypto** exemption for qualifying capital raises. [September 10 substitute, sections 10102 to 10104](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)
- The SEC would retain authority over securities and investment-contract offers. The CFTC would receive exclusive jurisdiction over spot or cash digital-commodity transactions conducted through entities registered, or required to register, with it. The draft says most network-token sales outside an originator or underwriter’s investment-contract offering are digital-commodity sales. [September 10 substitute, section 20201, PDF pages 460 to 464](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)

### Market and customer protections

- The CFTC framework covers digital-commodity exchanges, brokers, dealers, custodians, pool operators, and trading advisers. The Agriculture Committee describes customer fund segregation, conflict-of-interest safeguards, disclosures, registration, SEC-CFTC coordination, software-developer protection, and CFTC funding as core elements of the market-structure framework merged into CLARITY. [Senate Agriculture Committee](https://www.agriculture.senate.gov/newsroom/rep/press/release/boozman-leads-ag-committee-in-advancing-crypto-market-structure-legislation), [September 10 substitute, Division B](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)
- The draft applies Bank Secrecy Act and sanctions requirements to covered intermediaries, requires anti-fraud controls for digital-asset kiosks, provides for temporary holds requested by law enforcement in specified circumstances, and adds studies and programs on mixers, cybersecurity, and illicit finance. [Senate Banking majority summary](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/majority/the-facts-the-clarity-act-protects-main-street-unleashes-responsible-innovation-and-cracks-down-on-fraud-and-money-laundering), [September 10 substitute, Titles II, III, and IX](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)
- The draft prohibits a digital-asset service provider from paying interest or yield solely for holding a customer’s payment stablecoin balance, while permitting qualifying activity-based or transaction-based rewards. It directs the SEC, CFTC, and Treasury to write joint rules and requires a study of deposit effects on banks and credit unions. [September 10 substitute, section 10404, PDF pages 224 to 238](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)
- The draft preserves self-custody rights and contains protections for certain non-controlling software developers, but the scope of DeFi and illicit-finance obligations remains politically contested. [September 10 substitute, sections 10601, 10604, 10605, 20209, and 20216](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf), [Banking minority critique](https://www.banking.senate.gov/newsroom/minority/new-banking-minority-staff-analysis-identifies-five-major-loopholes-in-digital-asset-market-clarity-act)

## How the CLARITY Act could affect XRP

### 1. XRP already has meaningful federal regulatory clarity

The article should not imply that XRP is presently waiting for CLARITY to become a commodity. Effective March 23, 2026, a joint SEC interpretation with CFTC guidance expressly listed XRP among examples of **digital commodities** and said digital commodities themselves are not securities. The release also says the interpretation does not replace the binding Howey test and describes the classification based on the asset’s characteristics and functions at that time. [SEC interpretive release page](https://www.sec.gov/rules-regulations/2026/03/s7-2026-09), [Federal Register version hosted by CFTC, lines 610 to 648](https://www.cftc.gov/LawRegulation/FederalRegister/finalrules/2026-05635.html)

That agency interpretation can guide current enforcement and compliance, but it is not the same as a statute passed by Congress. CLARITY’s XRP relevance is therefore greater durability, a complete market-supervision framework, and express treatment of prior court and ETP facts, rather than an overnight switch from “security” to “commodity.”

### 2. The September draft contains unusually direct protection for XRP

Section 10105(b)(1) says that if a non-appealable final federal judgment entered before enactment found a digital-asset transaction was not an offer, sale, or distribution of a security, the asset transferred in that transaction is not considered a security under the listed securities laws. The final Ripple judgment remains in effect after both sides dismissed their appeals on August 7, 2025. [September 10 substitute, section 10105(b)(1), PDF pages 114 to 115](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf), [SEC litigation release on dismissal](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26369)

Section 10105(b)(2) separately says a network token is neither an ancillary asset nor a security if, on January 1, 2026, it was the principal asset of an exchange-traded product that was not registered under the Investment Company Act and whose shares traded on a national securities exchange. This appears to cover XRP. The Canary XRP ETF began trading on Nasdaq on November 12, 2025, held XRP as its principal exposure, and was not registered under the Investment Company Act. This is a legal-text inference, not an official XRP-specific determination by Congress. [September 10 substitute, section 10105(b)(2), PDF page 115](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf), [Canary XRP ETF 2025 Form 10-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/2039505/000199937126007350/xrpc-10k_123125.htm)

### 3. The Ripple decision’s transaction distinction still matters

The draft does not simply erase the Ripple case. The district court found Ripple’s institutional sales were unregistered investment-contract offerings, while other secondary offers and sales did not violate Section 5. After the appeals were dismissed, the $125,035,150 penalty and injunction remained in effect. The draft’s prior-ruling protection applies to transactions found not to be securities; it does not reverse the unfavorable institutional-sales finding. [SEC litigation release](https://www.sec.gov/enforcement-litigation/litigation-releases/lr-26369), [SEC Commissioner Crenshaw statement summarizing the district court findings](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/crenshaw-statement-ripple-050825)

For future Ripple distributions, the bill’s core model matters: XRP can be a non-security network token while a particular capital-raising offer involving XRP can still be an investment contract that triggers SEC disclosure or registration rules. [September 10 substitute, section 4B(b), PDF pages 25 to 28](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)

### 4. XRP trading and institutional access could gain a national market framework

If enacted and implemented, U.S. platforms handling XRP spot trades could operate under a CFTC registration and customer-protection framework rather than relying on a patchwork of enforcement positions and state rules. Clearer listing, brokerage, custody, and capital pathways could reduce legal friction for institutions and intermediaries that support XRP. The exact commercial effect would depend on final language, agency rulemaking, registration timelines, and firms’ compliance choices. [September 10 substitute, sections 20103 to 20219](https://www.lummis.senate.gov/wp-content/uploads/EHF26718.pdf)

Do not convert that into a price prediction. Passage would not guarantee an XRP rally, new bank adoption, higher network use, or immediate regulatory implementation. Markets may anticipate the result, and many operative details require rules after enactment.

## What to expect and what to watch

1. **The roll-call total.** Sixty affirmative votes are normally needed. The most important immediate signal is whether a bipartisan coalition supports opening the floor process.
2. **The label on the vote.** Report it as “cloture on the motion to proceed.” Avoid headlines that say the Senate “passed CLARITY” after a successful cloture vote.
3. **The motion to proceed.** If cloture succeeds, watch when the post-cloture time is yielded back or expires and when the Senate votes to take up the bill.
4. **Which substitute is actually offered.** The September 10 PDF is proposed language. A manager’s amendment or other floor amendment can alter it.
5. **Further cloture.** One successful cloture vote does not eliminate the possibility of another 60-vote test on the substitute, amendments, or the bill.
6. **Ethics enforcement.** Track which officials and family members are covered, whether state attorneys general receive enforcement authority, and whether the enacted provision changes from the September draft.
7. **Stablecoin rewards and bank deposits.** Watch the line between prohibited passive yield and permitted activity-based rewards, plus Treasury’s authority and the mandated deposit-flight analysis.
8. **DeFi, mixers, and the Bank Secrecy Act.** Watch how the final text distinguishes a decentralized protocol from a controlled intermediary and whether late changes satisfy law-enforcement concerns.
9. **Federal preemption.** The balance between one federal framework and state consumer or securities authority is a central policy dispute.
10. **Bicameral reconciliation.** Senate passage would not send this substitute directly to the president. The House and Senate must agree to identical text.
11. **Implementation timing.** Many provisions require SEC, CFTC, Treasury, FinCEN, and banking-regulator rules. Legal enactment and operational compliance would not happen simultaneously.
12. **XRP reaction versus XRP fundamentals.** Separate price volatility around the vote from actual changes to exchange access, custody, institutional participation, Ripple distribution practices, or XRPL usage.
